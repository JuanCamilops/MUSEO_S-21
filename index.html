The History of S-21

S-21, now known as the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum, is located in Phnom Penh, the capital of Cambodia. Before becoming a prison, it was a high school.

However, between 1975 and 1979, during the Khmer Rouge regime led by Pol Pot, it was transformed into a detention, interrogation, and torture center. Prisoners lived in extremely harsh conditions: they were chained, isolated, and subjected to violent interrogations.

Today, S-21 is a public museum. Its main focus is to educate visitors about the history of the genocide, remember the victims, and promote awareness about human rights.

What to See

The site is divided into main blocks. Visitors can see the original prison cells, photographs of the victims, and historical documents.

Building A

Rooms where main interrogations took place, preserving the original conditions of the facility.

Building B

Impactful galleries that house the photographic records of thousands of imprisoned people.

Building C

Area subdivided into small wooden and brick cells used for extreme isolation.

Building D

Spaces designated for education, documentation, testimonies, and silent reflection.
